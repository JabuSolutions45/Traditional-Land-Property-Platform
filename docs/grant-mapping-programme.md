# Umeli national mapping programme: grant notes

## Purpose of these notes

This document groups potential funding components for future grant proposals. It is not a final funding application, budget, legal opinion or commitment to a government partner. Each proposal can select the relevant workstreams and adapt them to the funder's mandate.

## 1. Phase-one location capability

For the first release, the official Municipal Demarcation Board 2026 ward geography is considered sufficient to:

- Place seller-supplied coordinates within a province, district, municipality and ward.
- Provide a stable administrative reference for a property listing.
- Suggest a village or settlement from the Umeli place directory.
- Allow the seller to confirm or correct the suggested local name.

The ward layer does not, by itself, establish village, traditional-jurisdiction or individual property boundaries. The interface must describe a village result as suggested or likely unless supported by a reliable boundary dataset.

## 2. National village and settlement mapping

### Purpose

Develop a current, auditable national directory of villages, suburbs, townships, informal settlements and locally recognised subareas that can be used alongside official municipal geography.

### Proposed activities

- Digitise village and settlement boundaries where reliable boundaries do not exist.
- Record formal names, local-language names, alternative spellings and historical names.
- Map recognised public landmarks such as schools, clinics, police stations and government facilities.
- Reconcile community descriptions with municipal, provincial and national datasets.
- Record disputed, overlapping or uncertain boundaries without forcing a false single answer.
- Retain the source, collection date, confidence level and review status of every boundary.

### Public value

The work could help close gaps in rural and informal-settlement geographic data while supporting service planning, emergency response, address improvement and local economic participation.

## 3. Traditional-leadership jurisdiction mapping

### Purpose

Map the areas associated with recognised traditional-leadership institutions independently of municipal boundaries.

### Hierarchy to record, where applicable

1. King or queen.
2. Principal traditional leader.
3. Senior traditional leader or chief.
4. Headman or headwoman.
5. Traditional council and authorised nominees.

Local titles must be preserved and interpreted in their provincial and customary context.

### Proposed information

- Institution, traditional council and recognised office bearer.
- Leadership level and reporting relationship.
- Government recognition source, date and effective period.
- Appointment, acting, regency, vacancy or disputed status.
- Villages and headman areas served.
- Jurisdiction boundary or approximate area of responsibility.
- Boundary source, confidence level and review history.
- Areas of overlap, uncertainty or dispute.

Government recognition or remuneration of an office bearer may support the existence of an official relationship, but it does not necessarily prove the precise geographic limits of that person's jurisdiction. Recognition, remuneration, community confirmation and geographic coverage must therefore be recorded as separate facts.

## 4. Community-contributed continuous improvement

### Purpose

Allow sellers, residents, traditional authorities and other authorised contributors to improve incomplete location information over time.

### Controls

- Keep official source data separate from community-contributed information.
- Treat corrections as pending until reviewed.
- Never silently overwrite an official or historical record.
- Record the contributor, evidence, date, coordinates and reason for the correction.
- Permit alternative or disputed claims to coexist while under review.
- Use role-based review by trained nominees, municipalities, traditional councils or platform data administrators.
- Publish only the confidence and status justified by the available evidence.

Possible contributions include missing village names, spelling corrections, local aliases, landmarks, headman-area associations and proposed boundary adjustments.

## 5. GIS equipment and field-mapping workstream

### Purpose

Equip trained field teams to collect consistent geographic evidence for village areas, traditional jurisdictions and the approximate extent of sellers' properties.

### Potential funded items

- Survey-grade or mapping-grade GNSS/GPS devices selected for the required accuracy.
- Rugged smartphones or tablets with offline mapping capability.
- Power banks, solar charging equipment and protective cases.
- Mobile-data and fieldwork costs.
- GIS software, secure cloud storage and data-backup services.
- Field safety equipment and transport.
- Device calibration, maintenance, replacement and asset management.
- Independent quality assurance and specialist survey support.

Equipment specifications and procurement should follow a documented accuracy requirement rather than assuming that every GPS device provides survey-quality results.

## 6. Training traditional leaders and nominated field representatives

### Purpose

Develop local capacity to collect reliable, repeatable and well-governed geographic evidence.

### Proposed training

- Basic map reading, coordinate capture and boundary walking.
- Correct use and care of GNSS/GPS equipment.
- Offline data collection and synchronisation.
- Recording landmarks, access routes and locally recognised names.
- Capturing evidence, photographs, dates, participants and consent.
- Distinguishing an approximate occupation area from a legally surveyed cadastral boundary.
- Handling overlapping claims, objections and unresolved disputes.
- Privacy, personal safety and protection of restricted property coordinates.
- Quality checks, error estimates and escalation to GIS or surveying professionals.
- Version control and preservation of the original field observations.

Training should include practical assessment, refresher sessions and a defined process for appointing, suspending and replacing authorised nominees.

## 7. Seller-property boundary estimates

### Phase-one position

A seller may provide:

- A GPS point representing the property location.
- An approximate outline drawn on a map.
- A locally used stand or allocation reference.
- Photographs and nearby recognised landmarks.
- Supporting customary or administrative documents.

### Required wording and safeguards

Any outline collected by a seller, traditional leader, nominee or mapping device must be labelled **approximate property extent** unless it has been produced or confirmed through the legally required professional and cadastral process.

The platform must not represent an approximate outline as:

- A cadastral boundary.
- Proof of ownership or title.
- A registered survey diagram.
- Resolution of an overlapping claim or dispute.

Where a legally authoritative property boundary is required, the matter must be referred to an appropriately registered professional and the relevant government process.

## 8. Data-quality framework

Each geographic record should retain:

- Geometry type: point, line or polygon.
- Collection method and equipment used.
- Reported or measured positional accuracy.
- Collector and supervising organisation.
- Collection and effective dates.
- Source documents and participants.
- Review status and reviewer.
- Confidence classification.
- Objections, disputes and competing versions.
- Relationship to official municipal, cadastral and traditional geography.

Suggested confidence descriptions include:

- Official administrative boundary.
- Professionally surveyed or cadastral source.
- Authority-confirmed local boundary.
- Community-reviewed estimate.
- Contributor-submitted estimate.
- Unreviewed or disputed.

## 9. Potential grant categories and purposes

### Digital inclusion and rural access

Improve access to trustworthy property and location information for communities with limited formal addressing and digital infrastructure.

### Spatial-data infrastructure

Build reusable, interoperable datasets connecting municipal, village, traditional and community geography.

### Traditional-governance capacity

Equip and train recognised institutions and nominees to participate in transparent, auditable digital processes.

### Land-administration improvement

Create better evidence and referral pathways without claiming to replace cadastral surveying, deeds registration or lawful land-administration processes.

### Youth skills and employment

Train local field mappers, data stewards and community verification teams under professional supervision.

### Public-service planning

Improve links between communities and schools, clinics, police stations, roads and other official facilities.

### Research and policy

Produce evidence about geographic data gaps, settlement change, traditional-jurisdiction overlaps and the practical needs of customary-land communities.

## 10. Possible implementation phases

1. **Pilot:** Giyani, Malamulele and Venda; test equipment, training, consent and review procedures.
2. **Provincial expansion:** Extend to Limpopo with provincial and traditional-governance partners.
3. **Multi-province programme:** Prioritise areas with customary tenure and major data gaps.
4. **National programme:** Establish common standards, shared governance and long-term maintenance.

Each phase should include independent data-quality assessment, community feedback and a documented decision on whether expansion is justified.

## 11. Potential partners

Subject to formal agreement and verification, relevant partners may include:

- Municipal Demarcation Board.
- Department of Traditional Affairs and provincial traditional-affairs departments.
- Traditional councils and houses of traditional and Khoi-San leaders.
- Municipal planning and GIS units.
- Chief Surveyor-General and provincial surveyor-general offices.
- Statistics South Africa.
- Relevant land-reform, rural-development and human-settlements institutions.
- Universities, GIS research groups and professional surveying bodies.
- Schools, clinics, police services and other custodians of public-facility data.
- Community organisations and trained local data stewards.

## 12. Funding-proposal safeguards

Every proposal should:

- Avoid claiming that Umeli determines ownership, legal title or final boundaries.
- Obtain informed participation and appropriate authority before field collection.
- Protect exact property coordinates and sensitive personal information.
- Budget for governance, maintenance, verification and dispute handling—not only devices.
- Define ownership, licensing, access and permitted reuse of funded data.
- Provide for offline operation and low-cost Android devices.
- Require an audit trail and preservation of source history.
- Establish how government and community partners can correct information.
- Include professional surveying referrals where legal precision is required.

## 13. Government, bank and professional handover pathway

### Purpose

Umeli should create structured preliminary location and property evidence that can later be reviewed, corrected and upgraded by government institutions, banks, valuers, conveyancers and registered surveying professionals. It should not become a closed mapping system that partners must reconstruct from the beginning.

### Design requirements

- Use documented, widely supported geographic formats and coordinate-reference systems.
- Provide controlled exports through authorised processes rather than locking data into one map provider.
- Preserve original coordinates, field observations, evidence, contributors and collection methods.
- Record the accuracy, confidence and legal status of every point or boundary.
- Keep community estimates separate from professionally surveyed and cadastral records.
- Maintain stable identifiers so later survey results can be linked to the original property record.
- Version boundaries instead of overwriting earlier estimates.
- Record which professional or institution supplied, reviewed or accepted an upgraded boundary.
- Support integration with relevant government, financial and professional systems subject to approval, privacy law and access controls.
- Obtain appropriate consent and authority before sharing seller or property information.

### Property progression

A property record may progress through clearly described evidence levels:

1. Seller-supplied location point.
2. Seller-drawn approximate property extent.
3. Community or traditional-authority field estimate.
4. Trained GIS review.
5. Professional valuation or due-diligence record.
6. Registered survey or authoritative cadastral reference, where applicable.

Progression to a higher evidence level must add a new record and must not erase the earlier evidence. Reaching one level does not automatically prove ownership, transferability, bankability or legal title.

### Funding objective

The long-term objective is to make properties easier for authorised institutions to locate, assess and investigate for lawful funding or development. Better geographic evidence may reduce the cost of field discovery and due diligence, but lending and funding decisions remain with regulated financial institutions and their approved professional processes.

## 14. Core proposal statement

Umeli proposes a phased, community-informed national mapping programme that connects official ward geography with villages, informal settlements, public landmarks and traditional-leadership jurisdictions. The programme will combine authoritative sources, trained local field collection and moderated community contributions. It will improve geographic evidence and access to services while clearly distinguishing administrative boundaries, community estimates and legally surveyed property boundaries. Its interoperable records will provide a practical starting point that authorised government, financial and surveying partners can review and progressively upgrade rather than recreating the location history from the beginning.
